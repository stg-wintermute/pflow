1.  Overview

    The populated corpus covers, with depth and peer-reviewed primary sources:
    (a) the CFG+dominators+def-use substrate pflow already has; (b) the PDG/SDG
    family and program slicing built on top of it; (c) slice-based cohesion and
    function-decomposition refactoring; (d) control-flow complexity metrics
    including essential complexity (the "how structured is this?" measure that
    bears directly on clean regeneration); and (e) Python-specific CFG
    construction (CPython bytecode) and flow-native bug detection.

    What it does NOT cover is the entire "value-flow / functional IR" axis of
    the maintainer's question (SSA and beyond) and the codegen/scheduling side
    of regeneration (RVSDG/Sea-of-Nodes scheduling, polyhedral codegen, SSA
    destruction). The corpus answers "CFG vs PDG, and how to get from a
    dependence graph back to clean structured source" very well; it cannot speak
    to "SSA vs RVSDG vs Sea-of-Nodes" at all.


2.  Key Findings

    2.1.  The current substrate (CFG + dominators + def-use) is the CORRECT
          BASE LAYER, and the corpus treats it as the thing everything else is
          BUILT FROM rather than an alternative to other IRs.

          Every higher structure in the corpus is defined as a function of CFG +
          (post-)dominators + reaching definitions:
            - Control dependence = post-dominator frontier on the reverse CFG;
              PDG construction Step 1-3 takes "Input: CFG of a procedure" and
              computes the post-dominator tree, then derives control-dependence
              edges. [ref: pdg-slicing-research.miner:1]
            - Data dependence edges = "Standard reaching definitions / def-use
              chains." [ref: flow-complexity-metrics.miner:13]
            - The pflow metric table explicitly computes slice cohesion, control-
              dependence fan-out, DepDegree, live-range, essential complexity,
              etc. all from "CFG + def-use + post-dominators / dominators."
              [ref: flow-complexity-metrics.miner:29]
          So the maintainer's instinct to keep the CFG form is well supported:
          the corpus never proposes replacing the CFG; it proposes LAYERING a
          dependence graph on top of it.
          Confidence: HIGH (consistent across pdg-slicing:1, flow-complexity:13,
          flow-complexity:29, and the pflow algorithm sketch slice-cohesion:49)

    2.2.  For ANALYSIS (slicing, cohesion, dependence questions), the corpus
          recommends the PDG, because it turns slicing from iterative dataflow
          into a single graph-reachability traversal.

          Weiser's original slice is an iterative backward dataflow fixpoint over
          the CFG, O(V^2 * N). [ref: pdg-slicing-research.miner:3]
          Ottenstein & Ottenstein showed a backward slice = "all vertices w such
          that there exists a directed path from w to [criterion] v in the PDG,"
          reducing slicing to BFS/DFS, O(V + E) in the size of the PDG.
          [ref: pdg-slicing-research.miner:2]
          The PDG bundles BOTH control and data edges so a single backward
          traversal is semantically correct; a data-edges-only traversal is
          provably WRONG (Weiser's if/print(y) example: the predicate must be in
          the slice or the assignment runs unconditionally).
          [ref: pdg-slicing-research.miner:3]
          IMPLICATION FOR pflow: pflow already has the inputs (CFG, post-doms,
          def-use). Building the PDG is a derivation, not a new front end. The
          pflow algorithm sketch already assumes this: "build PDG from pflow's
          CFG + def-use, then do backward reachability."
          [ref: slice-cohesion-refactoring.miner:49]
          Confidence: HIGH (three primary peer-reviewed sources agree:
          pdg-slicing:1, :2, :3)

    2.3.  For INTERPROCEDURAL analysis, the upgrade from PDG is the SDG (System
          Dependence Graph), but it carries real construction cost and a
          correctness hazard pflow would have to handle.

          The SDG adds entry/call/actual-in/actual-out/formal-in/formal-out
          vertices plus parameter and summary edges to connect per-procedure
          PDGs. Slicing becomes a TWO-PASS traversal to avoid "unrealizable
          paths" (entering a procedure from one call site and exiting to a
          different one). [ref: pdg-slicing-research.miner:4]
          COST: summary-edge computation is O(n^3) preprocessing (CFL-reachability
          / attribute grammar), then O(n^2) per slice query.
          [ref: pdg-slicing-research.miner:4]
          So intraprocedural PDG slicing is cheap (linear), but going whole-
          program is a cubic-preprocessing commitment.
          Confidence: HIGH (Horwitz-Reps-Binkley primary, corroborated by the
          duplicate SDG record slice-cohesion-refactoring.miner:19/:41)

    2.4.  For SIMPLIFICATION (pflow's primary mission), the recommended unit is
          the slice + the SESE region, derived from PDG/dominators - NOT a new
          IR.

          The function-decomposition pipeline the corpus converges on:
            - Compute decomposition slices per output variable (backward
              reachability on the PDG). [ref: slice-cohesion-refactoring.miner:49]
            - Cluster output variables by pairwise slice overlap (Jaccard) to
              find natural splits. [ref: slice-cohesion-refactoring.miner:18]
            - Grow a seed to the minimal single-entry/single-exit (SESE) region
              that is actually extractable - the "Wedge" step. The SESE
              constraint is what makes extraction valid; "arbitrary subsets of
              statements cannot be extracted because they may not form valid
              control flow." [ref: slice-cohesion-refactoring.miner:9]
            - An alternative non-slice route: successive edge contraction on a
              method-local dependence graph ("contract strongly-coupled
              statements into atomic segments, then find optimal segment
              groupings"). [ref: slice-cohesion-refactoring.miner:43]
          The reference Python implementation in this space (acheshkov/
          program_slicing) layers exactly CFG -> CDG -> DDG -> PDG (= CDG + DDG),
          all as networkx DiGraphs, on tree-sitter - i.e. the same layering this
          synthesis recommends, in Python. [ref: slice-cohesion-refactoring.miner:27]
          Confidence: HIGH for the slice+SESE approach (Lakhotia/Deprez,
          Gold/Harman, pflow sketch agree); MEDIUM for edge-contraction as an
          alternative (single preprint source, slice-cohesion:43).

    2.5.  For REGENERATION to clean source, the corpus's answer is CONTROL-FLOW
          STRUCTURING measured by ESSENTIAL COMPLEXITY - a CFG-reduction
          technique - NOT a value-IR scheduling technique.

          The corpus's only grounded regeneration-quality signal is essential
          complexity ev(G): iteratively collapse single-entry/single-exit
          (structured) subgraphs to single nodes; whatever survives is the
          irreducible, "unstructurable" residue. ev(G)=1 means fully structured
          (cleanly regenerable); ev(G)>1 counts the goto-like tangles that
          "CANNOT be replaced by structured equivalents without adding boolean
          flags or duplicating code." [ref: flow-complexity-metrics.miner:2]
          "ev(G) - 1 = number of unstructured 'excess' control flows to
          eliminate"; "essential complexity = 1 is the refactoring target."
          [ref: flow-complexity-metrics.miner:2]
          The Ada/gnatmetric variant operationalizes this for a real language:
          early return/raise/goto out of a compound statement count as
          non-structural; short-circuit boolean forms do not.
          [ref: flow-complexity-metrics.miner:21]
          IMPLICATION: For pflow's regeneration goal, the corpus says the lever
          is "reduce ev(G) to 1 by structuring the CFG," computed on the SAME
          CFG+dominators pflow already has. It says NOTHING about whether a
          value-IR (RVSDG/Sea-of-Nodes) would make regeneration easier - that is
          precisely the gap (see 2.7).
          Confidence: HIGH for ev(G) as the structuring metric; the claim that
          this is *the* regeneration strategy is the corpus's framing, but it is
          only one half of the regeneration problem (control structuring) and
          says nothing about data/value scheduling.

    2.6.  pflow's existing front end is Python-source / CPython-bytecode shaped,
          and the corpus documents that this CFG form is the natural fit for
          Python.

          CPython itself uses a basic-block CFG (basicblock struct, b_next,
          exception stack per block) - the same shape pflow uses.
          [ref: bytecode-cfg-research.miner:7]
          The Python-native def-use option (beniget) builds explicit def->use
          edges on a generic AST but is explicitly "non-data-dependent" (no path-
          sensitivity; "conditional assignments don't guarantee definitions") and
          cannot handle eval/exec/star-imports/globals().
          [ref: static-analysis-detectors-research.miner:33]
          The flow-native bug detectors pflow targets (use-before-def,
          unreachable code, resource-leak post-dominance) are classified as
          requiring exactly "CFG, reaching definitions, dominators, post-
          dominators, liveness." [ref: static-analysis-detectors-research.miner:50]
          IMPLICATION: The analysis and detection workloads are satisfied by the
          current substrate + a derived PDG. There is no evidence in the corpus
          that a lower/different IR is needed for the ANALYSIS jobs.
          Confidence: HIGH

    2.7.  [NO CORPUS EVIDENCE] The value-flow IR family - the heart of the
          maintainer's "is there a better LEVEL?" question - is entirely absent.

          SSA, RVSDG, Sea-of-Nodes, CPS/ANF, e-graphs/equality-saturation, and
          the polyhedral model return ZERO substantive hits (see section 0). The
          corpus therefore CANNOT tell you:
            - whether SSA/phi-functions would simplify pflow's def-use/dataflow
              (the classic argument: SSA gives each value one definition, making
              def-use trivial and reaching-defs implicit);
            - whether RVSDG or Sea-of-Nodes - which deliberately under-specify
              control order and re-derive a schedule - would make clean-source
              REGENERATION easier or harder than CFG structuring;
            - whether SSA destruction quality (the regeneration-from-SSA problem)
              is a concern;
            - whether e-graphs/equality saturation are worth it for rewrite-based
              simplification;
            - whether the polyhedral model buys anything for Python (almost
              certainly out of scope for general Python, but the corpus does not
              say).
          This is the single most important gap relative to the question asked.
          Confidence: N/A - this is an absence, not a finding.

6.  CONFLICTS

    No hard source-vs-source contradictions were found in the grounded material;
    the PDG/slicing/cohesion literature is mutually citing and consistent. Two
    TENSIONS worth the maintainer's attention (not contradictions):

    6.1.  Slicing efficiency: Weiser's CFG-based iterative slice is O(V^2 * N)
          [ref: pdg-slicing-research.miner:3] vs PDG reachability O(V+E)
          [ref: pdg-slicing-research.miner:2]. Not a conflict - the second
          supersedes the first - but it is the concrete reason to add the PDG
          layer rather than slice on the raw CFG.

    6.2.  Naming collision to avoid in any RFC: "SDG" means TWO different things
          in this corpus. Horwitz-Reps-Binkley SDG = System Dependence Graph
          (interprocedural). [ref: pdg-slicing-research.miner:4] Tiwari/Joshi SDG
          = Structure Dependence Graph (method-local, for edge-contraction
          extraction). [ref: slice-cohesion-refactoring.miner:43] The latter
          record flags this collision itself.

    The largest "conflict" is really the GAP: the question presupposes a
    comparison (CFG vs SSA vs RVSDG vs Sea-of-Nodes vs ...) that the corpus can
    only half-populate. That should be resolved by mining, not by inference.
